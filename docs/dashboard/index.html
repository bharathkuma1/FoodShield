FoodShield: wheat price-stress early warning (Ukraine)

Does adding weather, production and trade signals warn of a ≥10% real farm-gate price rise within 3 months earlier or better than watching prices alone? Batch processing in Apache Spark; a local Kafka replay into Spark Structured Streaming shows the alerting path.

M0–M4: backtest and test disagree

F1 with 90% bootstrap intervals. On 2014–2020 every added signal made warnings worse than price-only M0; on 2021–2022 M4 beat M0 beyond noise. Eleven stress episodes in total are too few to settle it. The price + season control raised no alerts in 2021–2022, so its test F1 is 0.

What if? Nudge one month's inputs

Pick a month, then move the sliders. The scores come from the models frozen for the one-time test (trained on 2011–2017, alert lines chosen on 2018–2020). Inputs you don't move keep that month's real values. This shows how the models react; it is not a forecast, and the scores are rankings, not calibrated probabilities.

Plays back the alert log of a run recorded on the team laptop, month by month. Latency is the time from a month's last input reaching Kafka to its alert being written.

All streaming runs (Week 6 and Week 7)

1,864,486 records (daily weather for 232 grid cells, monthly price, annual production and trade) replayed in availability order through Kafka 4.4.0 into Spark Structured Streaming. Targets: ≥100 events/s sustained, ≥250 events/s burst, p95 alert latency ≤10 s.

Batch scaling (Week 7)

Fixed pipeline: daily cell weather → monthly per cell → wheat-weighted national series, with both outputs written as Parquet. Larger workloads are labelled synthetic copies of the real data (benchmark only). Median of 3 timed runs after a warm-up, 2 cores.
